A desktop app for shaker-driven modal testing with a USB NI cDAQ chassis (e.g. cDAQ-9174). It uses
continuous, synchronized analog input and analog output, and offers four excitation suites:
- a *random suite* (periodic random, multisine, chirp, burst, custom spectra),
- a *stepped-sine suite* with secant amplitude control (both give H1/H2/Hv FRFs, UFF 58 and TDMS),
- a *harmonic suite*: one base frequency with a list of harmonics (amplitude and phase each),
  fixed or controlled on a response channel by a quasi-Newton (Broyden) controller; it records
  time data only,
- a *phase-quadrature suite*: amplitude-dependent resonance. For every drive level the frequency
  is iterated to phase quadrature (or to maximum |H1|, reference-free), giving backbone curves.

the app runs in simulation mode).

* Workflow
1. *Channel Setup*: one row per sensor. Set the physical channel, name, *node* and *direction*
   (both are written to the UFF file), sensor type, sensitivity, units, range, IEPE current and coupling.
   Tick *Reference* on the drive-point force sensor. Every other enabled channel becomes one FRF.
   Sensor types: accelerometer, force, microphone and *pressure* (IEPE), strain gauge (bridge),
   voltage, and *Custom*. /Pressure (IEPE)/ reads an IEPE pressure transducer with its sensitivity
   in mV per kPa, Pa, bar, psi or MPa. /Custom/ is for any other voltage-output sensor (laser
   vibrometer, displacement probe, …): choose Custom, type the sensor's name in the field that
   appears, pick or type its unit (mm, µm, m/s, …) and enter the sensitivity in mV per that unit;
   set IEPE if it needs excitation. Pressure and custom channels are measured as voltage and scaled
   by the sensitivity; the custom name and unit are saved in the TDMS and UFF files (UFF type
   "pressure" for pressure, "general" for custom).
   Strain gauges use the bridge columns (bridge type, gage factor, excitation, nominal resistance,
   Poisson's ratio). The /Bulk edit/ menu numbers nodes, copies sensor settings to selected rows,
   and adds every channel of a module.
2. *Output Setup*: the AO channel, a drive voltage limit (the run is refused if any block would
   exceed it), the excitation type and RMS, the frequency band, sampling rate, block size,
   *dwell blocks*, *realizations* and the *IEPE decay time*. The panel on the right shows Δf,
   crest factor, peak against the limit, test duration and a preview of a drive block and its spectrum.
   Drag the divider to resize the settings column; the preview plots are resizable against each other.
3. *Measurement*: choose what to save, then press Start. You get:
   - *Channels A and B* are chosen once, above the tabs, and used by Live data, Controller and
     Harmonics. A is on the left axis, B on its own right axis, since units may differ; you can
     pick sensors, the drive or the trigger;
   - the live block of the two channels;
   - a *recent-blocks strip* showing the last /N/ blocks of those channels (default dwell + 2,
     adjustable up to 100) on the acquisition time axis, with dashed separators, block labels (=R3·2/4= = realization 3, dwell
     block 2 of 4) and the converged block used for the FRF shaded green. You can watch the
     transient die out and the response become periodic;
   - coherence, FRF magnitude and phase (H1/H2/Hv) from 0 to fs/2, updated after every
     realization. Dashed lines mark the band of interest;
   - progress, and an input-backlog health indicator;
   - *Export plots...*, which saves the live and FRF plots as PNG (2× resolution), SVG or PDF.
4. *Data Viewer*: point it at a run folder, or at a folder of runs (pick one from the /Run/
   list). The suite of every run is *detected automatically*, from =setup.json= or else the file
   names. Runs are tagged =[random]= / =[stepped]= in the lists, a badge shows the suite, and the
   stepped-only views appear only for stepped runs. It shows the run's settings and which files exist (H1/H2/Hv/Coherence UFF, converged
   blocks, full realizations, trigger log), then offers these views:
   - *Frequency domain*:
     - /Show: FRF/: coherence (or η), |H| and phase of any response over the full 0...fs/2 range
       (stepped sine: at the measured frequencies). /Source/ is either *Recomputed from TDMS*,
       calculated from the time data with the reference and labels from the channel table, or
       *Stored UFF files*, exactly as saved.
     - /Show: Amplitude spectrum/: the raw amplitude of any single channel. Random runs give the
       single-sided spectrum, RMS-averaged over the realizations' converged blocks. Stepped runs
       give the fundamental's amplitude at each excitation frequency.
   - *Harmonics (stepped sine only)*: the same harmonics page as on the Measurement tab (see
     /Harmonic decomposition/), computed from the run's dwell blocks. It follows the reference
     chosen on the channel table.
     - The dashed band markers are the excitation band (random) or the start and stop frequencies
       (stepped sine).
   - *Realizations*: pick a realization from the drop-down. You see either its converged block or,
     if saved, all of its dwell blocks, for two channels, with block separators, the converged
     block in green, and the amplitude spectrum of a chosen block (lin/log).
   - *Compare runs*: tick several runs (from the scanned folder, or add more with /Add runs from
     folder…/) and overlay the same channel from each. Channels are matched by name and each run
     uses its own reference. You can overlay an FRF (choose H1/H2/Hv; coherence or η on top,
     magnitude, phase) or an amplitude spectrum. *Random and stepped-sine runs can be compared
     directly*: stepped runs are drawn with markers at their measured frequencies, random runs as
     lines. The view exports as an image.
   - *Channels & re-processing*: a table of every channel (including the drive). Choose the
     *reference* (any channel, e.g. an accelerometer for transmissibilities, or the drive voltage)
     and which channels are *responses*, and edit *names, nodes and directions*. /Apply/
     recomputes H1/H2/Hv/coherence from the converged blocks. /Save re-processed copy.../ writes a
     new run folder =<run>_reprocessed_<time>= with the UFF files, the TDMS files carrying the new
     labels (optionally including every realization file), an updated =setup.json= (with a
     =reprocessing= note) and the trigger log. The original run is never modified. Runs saved
     without the converged-block TDMS can only be relabelled, and their reference stays fixed.
     Each plot view has its own *Export image...* button (PNG/SVG/PDF). A finished run is opened here
     automatically. *Stop* fades the drive out smoothly and still saves the
     realizations completed so far.

/File → Save setup/ stores everything as JSON. The last setup is restored automatically on startup.

* How a run works
The AI and AO tasks run in *continuous* mode from start to finish. AO regeneration is disabled, and
one new AO block is queued for every AI block read, so there are no gaps and no missed or repeated
samples. If the PC falls behind, DAQmx raises an error rather than dropping data silently. AO starts
on the AI start trigger, so AI block /k/ is the response to AO block /k/.

#+begin_example
[0 V for IEPE decay time] [fade-in] [R1 × dwell] [R2 × dwell] … [RR × dwell] [fade-out] [0 V]
#+end_example

- The *band* (f_min ... f_max) sets the lines excited by the band-limited types and the markers
  on the plots. It never limits what is saved: FRFs are always stored from Δf to fs/2.
- With multisine, Gaussian, chirp, burst and custom excitation every excited frequency is an exact
  FFT bin (k·fs/N), so there is no leakage.
- *Dwell blocks*: each random block is repeated /dwell/ times, so the response becomes periodic.
  The *last block of each realization* is the converged block. Only that block is used for the
  FRF average and saved to TDMS. The log reports the block-to-block change of the last two blocks
  in %; once the response has converged, this falls to the noise level.
- *Realizations* = number of averages. Use the Rectangular window for periodic excitation.
- *IEPE decay time*: the drive is held at 0 V while the IEPE conditioners settle. The data is
  displayed but discarded.

** Excitation types
All types are scaled to the requested RMS. All except burst random are periodic in the block.

| Type                           | Spectrum                                                    | Phases                           | Crest factor  | Use it for                                                                                      |
|--------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------+---------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Uniform random                 | white, 0 ... fs/2                                           | random (time samples)            | ≈ 1.7         | quick broadband checks                                                                          |
| Multisine random               | flat on every FFT line in the band                          | U(0, 2π)                         | ≈ 3–3.5       | general periodic-random FRFs                                                                    |
| Gaussian random (band-limited) | Rayleigh-distributed line amplitudes in the band            | U(0, 2π)                         | ≈ 3.5–4       | true random statistics, nonlinearity checks                                                     |
| Periodic chirp                 | flat in the band                                            | Schroeder (fast repeating sweep) | ≈ 1.6–2       | most force per amplifier volt, best SNR; deterministic, so averaging only reduces noise         |
| Burst random                   | band-limited random for x % of the block, then silence      | random                           | ≈ 3–3.5 (on)  | lightly damped structures: response decays inside the block, no window needed                   |
| Custom spectrum                | your table (f, value), as amplitude (linear) or PSD (log-log) | U(0, 2π)                         | depends       | shaped spectra: emphasise or avoid bands, follow a test specification                           |

*Custom spectrum*: enter the points in the table on the Output Setup tab. You can type them, paste
two columns from a spreadsheet (a header line is fine), import or export a CSV, or load a preset.
The plot under the table updates on every edit, with the table points in orange and the resulting
FFT-line amplitudes in blue. Values are either line amplitudes (linear interpolation) or a PSD
shape (log-log interpolation, i.e. straight dB/octave slopes, amplitude = √PSD). The spectrum is
zero outside the table's frequency range. Only the shape matters; the level comes from the RMS.

** FFT windows
Rectangular, Hann, Hamming, Blackman, Blackman-Harris, Flat top, Tukey (10 %) and Kaiser-Bessel
(β = 6), all periodic (DFT-even). *Rectangular is the right choice for every excitation here*:
the periodic types are leakage-free once the dwell blocks have converged, and burst random decays
inside the block. The other windows are for responses that are not periodic in the block. The log
notes when a non-rectangular window is used.

** FRF estimators (X = reference, Y = response)
#+begin_example
H1 = Gxy / Gxx                      (noise on the response)
H2 = Gyy / conj(Gxy)                (noise on the reference)
Hv = Gxy / (Gxx - lmin)             (total least squares; lmin = smallest eigenvalue of
                                     [Gxx  conj(Gxy); Gxy  Gyy]; Hv lies between H1 and H2)
coherence = |Gxy|^2 / (Gxx * Gyy)
with Gxx = <|X|^2>, Gyy = <|Y|^2>, Gxy = <conj(X) * Y>, averaged over the realizations.
#+end_example

* Stepped-sine suite
Choose *Suite → Stepped sine* on the Output Setup tab. The Measurement tab shows the chosen suite
as a badge and only the options, tabs and plots that apply to it; to switch suites, go back to
Output Setup. The shaker is
then driven with one sine at a time, stepping through the frequency plan. All the channel,
trigger, saving and viewer features work as in the random suite.

** Frequency plan
- *Frequencies* from start to stop, with a *linear step* (Hz) or *logarithmic* spacing (points
  per decade).
- Every frequency is snapped to an FFT line k·fs/N, so each block holds whole cycles. Blocks then
  join seamlessly, and the single-line DFT of a block has no leakage. Points closer together than
  Δf = fs/N are merged (the panel says how many); use a larger block for finer steps.
- *Settle after change*: blocks discarded after every change of frequency or drive level.
- *Dwell per frequency*: steady-state time analysed at each frequency (rounded up to whole blocks).

** Control
- *No control*: the same sine amplitude (V peak) at every frequency.
- *Secant control (closed loop)*: the chosen *control channel* is driven to the *target* (in that
  channel's units) within ± *tolerance*. The target is given either as
  - *RMS*: the block RMS, or
  - *Amplitude (RMS·√2)*: the equivalent single-harmonic amplitude, i.e. the peak of a pure sine
    with the same RMS. The controlled quantity is then block RMS × √2.
  - One drive amplitude per iteration. An iteration is /settle/ blocks plus one measured block.
    Because the output is queued /AO queue depth/ blocks ahead, the measured block is read that
    many blocks later; the drive is held meanwhile. A small queue (2) makes control faster.
  - Update rule (secant on f(A) = y(A) − target, using the last two valid points; the first
    step uses the origin, i.e. a proportional step):
    #+begin_example
    A_next = A_k − (y_k − y_target) · (A_k − A_(k−1)) / (y_k − y_(k−1))
    limited to  ½·A_k ≤ A_next ≤ 2·A_k   and   A_next ≤ drive limit
    #+end_example
    If the response did not grow with the drive (non-monotonic or noise), a proportional step is
    used instead.
  - The first frequency starts at the *initial drive*; every later frequency starts from the
    previous frequency's final drive.
  - *At least one refinement per frequency.* The first iteration at a frequency uses the
    carried-over drive and never counts as converged, even if it is already within tolerance:
    the drive is always corrected at least once from a measurement at this frequency (noted as
    /within tolerance: one refinement step/ in the iteration log). /Max iterations/ is therefore
    at least 2. A frequency can still end after one iteration when control fails outright, e.g.
    the drive limit is reached below the target.
  - The control must converge before the dwell starts. The test only moves to the next frequency
    after the dwell.

** Detected problems and what happens
| Condition                     | Detection                                                                          |
|-------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Input overload / saturation   | any channel's abs(peak) ≥ /overload %/ of its range; that block is not used for control |
| Target unreachable (overload) | the next secant step needs a drive at which a channel already overloaded            |
| Drive limit                   | drive at the limit and the target is still not reached                              |
| Max iterations                | not within tolerance after /max iterations/                                         |

*If control fails*, the setup decides:
- *Ask me*: the drive is held at a safe level (the best non-overloaded iteration) and a dialog
  offers /Retry/ (another set of iterations), /Accept best and continue/ (flagged), /Skip this
  frequency/, or /Stop the test/.
- *Accept best and continue (flagged)*, *Skip this frequency*, *Stop the test*: done automatically.

** Controller display (Measurement tab → Controller)
- Live status: step, frequency, phase, drive, control-channel RMS against target, error.
- *Current frequency* plot: control-channel RMS per iteration against the target ± tolerance band
  (green), drive per iteration (right axis), overloaded iterations marked ✕.
- *Sweep overview*: final drive against frequency (i.e. the inverse of the transfer from drive to
  control channel), final error %, flagged (○) and skipped or failed (✕) frequencies.
- *Table*: every frequency with status (colour-coded with semi-transparent tints that read well
  in light and dark themes), iterations, drive, achieved value, error, THD of the control and
  reference channels, the lowest fundamental fraction η, overloads and failure reason.
- FRFs (one point per frequency) and the fundamental fraction update after every frequency.
- *Harmonics* tab: the harmonic decomposition of the current block (see below).

** Fundamental fraction instead of coherence
Ordinary coherence is not useful in a stepped-sine test. Computed at the excitation line across
the dwell blocks, it only measures block-to-block repeatability: it is ≈ 1 for any steady sine,
exactly 1 with a single dwell block, and blind to distortion, because harmonics fall on other
lines. The stepped-sine suite therefore reports the *fundamental fraction*
#+begin_example
eta_channel = (power at the excitation line) / (total AC power of the block)   averaged over dwell blocks
eta_FRF     = eta_reference * eta_response
#+end_example
η = 1 for a clean, linear, steady response; it drops with noise, harmonic distortion and leftover
transients. It takes the place of coherence in the plots and is saved as
=<base>_FundamentalFraction.uff= (UFF 58, general real function). The random suite still uses
ordinary coherence.

** Harmonic decomposition
Each stepped-sine block holds whole cycles of f0, so it is an exact Fourier series in f0:
x(t) = a0 + Σ A_h cos(2π·h·f0·t + φ_h), with harmonic h on FFT line h·k (no window, no leakage).
- *Measurement → Harmonics* (live) and *Data Viewer → Frequency steps → Show: Harmonics* (any
  saved dwell block).
- Bar chart of the harmonic levels in dB relative to the fundamental for channels A and B, up to
  the chosen order (limited by Nyquist).
- One excitation cycle of channel A: measured samples, the Fourier series, the fundamental and the
  sum of harmonics 2…H.
- Text: fundamental amplitude and phase, THD, DC, the non-harmonic residual (noise and anything not
  at a harmonic) and the first harmonic levels.
- This per-block view is the *Block harmonics* tab on the Measurement tab; in the Data Viewer it is
  /Frequency steps → Show: Harmonics/.

In the Data Viewer the *Harmonics* page is built from the run's time data (dwell blocks, else the
converged block per frequency). Without time data it uses the saved =<base>_harmonics.csv=
(complex re/im; older files without those columns are rebuilt from amplitude ∠ phase), so the
harmonics can be viewed whenever either was saved; the page names its source. The same file feeds
/Frequency domain → Amplitude spectrum/ for such runs.

*Nyquist limits the measurable orders.* Harmonic h of f₀ can only be measured if h·f₀ < fs/2. At
fs = 5120 Hz (Nyquist 2560 Hz) and f₀ = 536 Hz that is h ≤ 4, whatever number is set. Output Setup
warns before the run (and the log repeats it), giving the highest measurable order and the
sampling rate that would allow all of them (e.g. 10 orders at 536 Hz need fs > 10.7 kHz: use 12.8
kHz on the NI 9234). The harmonics pages always list orders 1…n as set; orders that were above
Nyquist at every frequency of a run are greyed out and marked /above Nyquist, not measured/.

The Block harmonics readout lists every order up to the set number; orders above Nyquist at the
current frequency are omitted, and the readout says so.

** Strobed view (stepped sine)
Measurement tab → *Strobed view* (right): the latest block(s) of the current frequency folded onto
one period 0 … T = 1/f, as a point cloud with the fitted Fourier series (DC + harmonics 1…n) and
the residual in % of the AC RMS. Only blocks with the same drive are overlaid, so a settling
block right after a frequency change shows its transient as scatter around the fit. In the Data
Viewer: /Frequency steps/ page → /Show: Strobed view/ folds all dwell blocks of the chosen step.
The harmonic suite has the same view (see below).

** Harmonics against frequency (stepped sine only)
A separate page, *Harmonics vs frequency* (next to the FRF on the Measurement tab, and the
*Harmonics* sub-tab in the Data Viewer), shows how the harmonics evolve over the sweep:
- Tick *any number of channels* and *any set of harmonic orders* (1 = fundamental). The
  /Fundamental/, /Odd/ (3, 5, 7, …) and /Even/ (2, 4, 6, …) buttons tick or untick a whole group;
  a button shows pressed while every order of its group is ticked.
- /Show/: *Amplitude* |X_h| (channel units, optional log axis), *Level re own fundamental* (dBc),
  or *Harmonic FRF* |Y_h| / |X_1,ref| (harmonic h of each channel over the reference fundamental).
- Phase plot: phase of each harmonic relative to the excitation, φ_h − h·φ_ref,1, which does not
  depend on where the block starts.
- Colour identifies the channel; line style and marker identify the harmonic order. Orders with
  h·f0 ≥ fs/2 are omitted. The page exports as an image.
The FRF plots themselves always show the ordinary (fundamental) FRF.
In the Data Viewer the harmonics are recomputed from the run's time data: the dwell-block file if
it exists, otherwise the converged-block file (the page says which). A run saved without either
TDMS file has no time data to decompose; the page then says so instead of staying empty, and any
other problem is shown there as an error message.

** Saving harmonics
The number of harmonics is set *once*, on Output Setup → Stepped sine → /Harmonics: orders 1 … n/
(2–20, default 5). It applies to the Block harmonics display, the Harmonics vs frequency page and
the saved files; the Data Viewer uses the value stored with each run.

With /Harmonics → CSV (complex) + UFF/ (Measurement tab, stepped sine) a run also writes:
- =<base>_harmonics.csv=: every channel, every frequency, orders 1…n, as the *complex* peak
  amplitude X_h = =re= + j·=im= (so that x(t) contains Re{X_h·e^(j2π·h·f0·t)}), plus the
  magnitude, phase (°) and level relative to the channel's own fundamental (dBc).
- =<base>_Harmonic<h>.uff= for h = 2…n: the complex Y_h/X_1 of every response (UFF 58).

** Stepped-sine output files
| File                              | Contents                                                                          |
|-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =<base>_H1/H2/Hv.uff=             | UFF 58 at the measured frequencies (uneven spacing is written as such)          |

The two TDMS files are independent check boxes in the Measurement tab (row /Time data → TDMS/):
- /Converged block of every frequency/ — the "converged block" is the last control iteration's
  block that met the tolerance. If you accept the best iteration after a failure, that iteration's
  block is saved (status =accepted=); with /No control/ it is the first dwell block.
- /Dwell blocks of every frequency/ — the full dwell record per step.

Frequencies in all files are computed from the *actual* sample rate read back from DAQmx, so they
are correct when a delta-sigma module coerces the rate.

FRFs come from the dwell blocks: single-line spectra of every channel, then H1/H2/Hv across the
dwell blocks, plus the fundamental fraction η. In the
Data Viewer a stepped run lists its *frequency steps* instead of realizations, and *Channels &
re-processing* can re-reference, relabel and re-save it like a random run.

* Harmonic suite
Choose *Suite → Harmonic* on the Output Setup tab. The drive is periodic in one base frequency:
#+begin_example
v(t) = Σ_i a_i · cos(n_i·Ω·t + φ_i),     Ω = 2π·f0
#+end_example
** Setup (Output Setup → Harmonic excitation)
- *Base frequency f0*. It is moved to the nearest FFT line k·fs/N (shown below the field), so every
  block holds k whole periods: blocks join seamlessly and each harmonic is read leakage-free from
  line n_i·k. Every listed order must be below Nyquist.
- *Harmonics table*: order n (whole number ≥ 1, each once), amplitude a (peak), phase φ (°).
  Add/remove rows with the buttons.
- *Control*:
  - /No control/: the table is the drive in volts, exactly v(t) above. The drive's peak must be
    within the drive limit.
  - /Quasi-Newton control (Broyden)/: the table is the *target* for the harmonics of the *control
    channel* (amplitude in its units). An amplitude of 0 means /suppress this harmonic/ (e.g. to
    cancel shaker distortion at 2Ω, 3Ω).
- *Initial drive*: the largest harmonic's drive at the first iteration; the others are scaled to
  their targets, but at least 5 %, so every harmonic is probed. *Tolerance*, *Max iterations*
  (≥ 2), *If control fails*, *Input overload at* and *Settle after change* work as in the
  stepped-sine suite.
- *Converged test*: how many converged blocks are run before the excitation is switched off
  (the duration is shown next to it).
- *Harmonics shown: 1 … n*: orders used by the displays (Block harmonics, strobed-view Fourier fit);
  at least the highest order in the table. This is the suite's only harmonic-count setting.

** Control (Broyden's quasi-Newton method)
The unknowns are the complex drive harmonics V_i, as the real vector x = [Re V; Im V]. The
residual is r(x) = [Re(Y − T); Im(Y − T)], with Y the measured harmonics of the control channel and
T the targets. One iteration is /settle/ blocks plus one measured block at a fixed drive.
#+begin_example
first Jacobian   B0 = diagonal: each harmonic's own gain Y_i / V_i (no cross-coupling yet)
step             x+ = x − B⁻¹ · r
update           B ← B + (Δr − B·Δx)·Δxᵀ / (Δxᵀ·Δx)          (Broyden's "good" update)
#+end_example
The Broyden updates learn the cross-coupling between harmonics, e.g. a nonlinear shaker or structure
that turns drive at Ω into response at 2Ω. Safeguards:
- a harmonic's drive may at most double per step (small ones: relative to the largest);
- the drive waveform's peak stays within the drive limit (the step is shortened to reach it
  exactly; already at the limit and still short of the target ⇒ /drive limit/ failure);
- if the error grows by more than 1.5×, the step is halved back towards the previous drive.

*Error* of harmonic i: e_i = |Y_i − T_i| / s_i, with s_i = |T_i|, or the largest |T| for targets
below 10 % of the largest (e.g. suppressed harmonics). It is a complex error: 3 % ≈ 3 % in
amplitude and 1.7° in phase. *Converged* when every e_i ≤ tolerance, and never on the first
iteration (at least one refinement).

*Failures* (as in stepped sine): input overload before the target is reached (above it the
drive is halved), drive limit, input saturation (the target needs more drive than overloads an
input), and max iterations. Actions: /Ask me/ (drive held at a safe level; Retry / Accept best
and run the test / Stop), /Accept the best iteration and run the test (flagged)/, or /Stop/.

** Measurement tab (harmonic suite)
Only what applies is shown; there is no FRF.
- *Live data*: the latest block and the recent-blocks strip (green = converged test block).
- *Controller (harmonic)*: f0, phase, iteration, max error, drive peak; a table per harmonic with
  target, response (amplitude ∠ phase), error (green within tolerance) and drive; and the max
  error on a log scale over the control iterations (blue) and the test blocks (orange), with the
  tolerance as a dashed line.
- *Block harmonics*: bar chart and one-cycle Fourier series of the latest block.
- *Strobed view* (also in the stepped-sine suite): the samples of the latest block(s), folded
  onto one period, 0 … T = 2π/Ω, as a point cloud overlaid with the fitted Fourier series
  (DC + harmonics 1…n). /Overlay last N blocks/ adds earlier blocks with the same drive (they all
  start at phase 0). The residual of the fit, as a % of the AC RMS, shows how much is not periodic
  in Ω (noise, transients, sub-harmonics). Channels A and B get one plot each.

* Phase-quadrature suite (amplitude-dependent resonance)
Choose *Suite → Phase Quadrature* on the Output Setup tab. For each drive level (a sine of fixed
voltage) the frequency is adjusted until the response is at resonance, then the converged block is
recorded and the next level starts from that frequency. Plotting the resonance frequency against
the response (or force) amplitude gives the *backbone curve* of a nonlinear mode.

** Setup (Output Setup → Phase quadrature)
- *Drive levels*: min … max (V peak), as a /number of levels/ or a /step/; order /ascending/,
  /descending/ or /up, then down/ (shows hysteresis or jumps).
- *Initial frequency*: first guess of the resonance; later levels start from the previous level's
  resonance. *Search range*: control fails if the resonance would lie outside it.
- *Control*:
  - /Phase quadrature (secant on frequency)/: the phase of the response's fundamental relative to
    the reference's is driven to the *quadrature phase* (presets: acceleration/force +90°,
    velocity/force 0°, displacement/force −90°, or custom) within ± *phase tolerance*.
  - /Reference-free/: the frequency of maximum |H1| = |response / reference| is located to
    ± *frequency tolerance* (bracketing search with parabolic refinement). Without a reference
    channel, |response| per drive volt is maximised. For a damped mode the |H1| peak is not
    exactly at phase quadrature (the difference is of the order of the damping).
- *Response channel*; the *reference channel* is shown as set on Channel Setup (needed for
  quadrature).
- *First step*, *Max step* (Hz per iteration), *Max iterations*, *If control fails* (ask / accept
  best and continue / skip this level / stop), *Input overload at*, *Settle after change*
  (blocks discarded after every frequency or level change), *Converged blocks* (recorded at the
  converged frequency: averaged for the result and saved), *Harmonics: orders 1 … n*.

** How it works
- *Any frequency.* The drive is a phase-continuous sine at the exact frequency (not limited to
  FFT lines), and every block is analysed by a least-squares fit of DC + harmonics at that
  frequency against absolute time. This is unbiased with a non-integer number of periods per block,
  so the frequency resolution is not Δf = fs/N.
- *Quadrature secant*: e(f) = wrap(φ(f) − φ_target);
  f⁺ = f − e·(f − f_prev)/(e − e_prev) when the phase falls with frequency (as it does through a
  resonance); otherwise a fixed step towards quadrature (phase above target ⇒ raise f), scaled
  with |e|/45°. Steps are limited to ± max step and to the search range.
- The first iteration of a level never counts as converged (at least one refinement).
- Failures: input overload, search range exhausted, max iterations.

** Measurement tab (phase quadrature)
No FRF. Left: *Live data*, *Block harmonics* (least squares at the exact frequency), *Controller
(phase quadrature)*: level, drive, frequency, phase, error, the iterations of the current level

(blocks of the same frequency and level, folded with their absolute time) and *Resonance vs
amplitude*: the backbone (response amplitude, reference amplitude, drive level, |H1| or response
per volt, against the resonance frequency; blue = increasing level, red = decreasing), and |H1| and
its phase at resonance.

* External trigger (high-speed camera)
The trigger works in all suites. Its mode labels follow the selected suite:

| Random suite                                     | Stepped-sine suite                                    | Harmonic suite                     |
|--------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------|
| Once, when the input task starts                 | Once, at the start of the acquisition                 | Once, when the input task starts   |
| Start of the converged block of each realization | At the start of the converged (dwell) block at every frequency | Start of the converged test blocks |
| Start of the dwell blocks of each realization    | At the start of every frequency step                  | Start of every control iteration   |
| Frame sync                                       | Frame sync                                            | Frame sync                         |

Phase quadrature: once at the start; at the start of the converged block of every level; at the
start of every drive level; or frame sync.

Enable *External trigger* on the Output Setup tab to send a pulse to a camera or any other device.
The pulse goes out on either:

- *another AO channel* (.../aoN). It is added to the same AO task as the shaker drive, so it is
  sample-exact with the drive. Set the active level: 3.3 V is enough for TTL inputs (≥ 2 V reads
  as high). Both AO channels get one common output range, so they must fit the module: ±4.24 V on
  an NI 9260, ±10 V on an NI 9263. The app checks this before starting. On delta-sigma AO modules
  (NI 9260 family), the reconstruction filter delays the pulse and rounds its edges, so use pulses
  of 10 samples or more, or a DO line for clean edges.
- *a DO line* (.../portN/lineM). This needs a hardware-timed (correlated) DIO module such as the
  NI 9401. It runs as its own continuous task on the same clock and start trigger.

The trigger line is written block by block alongside the drive, on the same sample clock, so every
pulse starts on an exact, known sample index:

| Mode                                             | Pulse at                                                    |
|--------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Once, when the input task starts                 | sample 0 (the start of the acquisition)                     |
| Start of the converged block of each realization | first sample of the last dwell block                        |
| Start of the dwell blocks of each realization    | first sample of the first dwell block                       |
| *Frame sync*                                     | *one pulse per sample*, from sample 0 to the end of the run |

You can also set the pulse width (in samples, with the equivalent µs shown) and the polarity:
active-high for a rising-edge trigger, active-low for a falling-edge one. The idle level is set
before the run, so a falling edge at sample 0 also works.

** Frame sync (4th mode)
Frame sync does not generate a waveform. It routes the input task's own *sample clock* to a PFI
terminal on a hardware-timed DIO module, e.g. =/cDAQ1Mod4/PFI0= on an NI 9401. The pin gives one
pulse for every acquired sample, starting with sample 0, for as long as the run lasts. Connect it
to the camera's *external sync / frame clock* input instead of its trigger input. Every frame is
then exposed on a DAQ sample clock edge: *camera frame f = AI sample f*, with no drift and no
need to match the camera's fps by hand.

- Choose the *polarity* that matches the camera's sync input. The pulse width is fixed by the
  hardware, so check it against the camera's minimum sync pulse width on a scope.
- The NI 9401 sets direction per group of 4 lines. Lines used as output (DO or PFI out) must be in
  an output group. Its outputs are 5 V TTL.
- The camera records frames for the whole run, including the IEPE decay time. Allow for that in its
  memory, or start camera recording with its own trigger input.
- The delta-sigma filter-delay caveat below still applies (frame f = AI sample f + filter delay).
- The pulses come out continuously, so =trigger_log.csv= is not written. The TDMS root properties
  record =frame_sync_terminal=.

What gets recorded (single-pulse modes):

- *=<base>_trigger_log.csv=* holds the pulse number, the *absolute sample index* (0 = AI start),
  the time, the block, the realization and the dwell block. If the camera fps equals the sampling
  rate, frame /f/ after pulse /p/ corresponds to sensor sample =sample_index[p] + f=.
- The TDMS groups contain a *=Trigger=* channel, and their =first_sample_index= and
  =first_sample_time_s= properties place every saved block on the same absolute sample axis.

Alignment caveats:

- *The camera clock is independent* in the single-pulse modes. A start pulse fixes frame 0, but
  the camera's own clock and the DAQ clock drift slightly apart, by tens of ppm. Use *Frame sync*
  to remove the drift completely.
- *Delta-sigma filter delay.* On delta-sigma input modules (NI 9234 etc.) each AI sample is
  delayed by the module's filter group delay (about 38 samples plus a few µs on the 9234; see its
  manual). The pulse leaves the output with almost no delay. So camera frame /f/ lines up with AI
  sample /f + delay/. If you need sub-sample certainty, also wire the trigger line into a spare AI
  channel: then the pulse is recorded with the same delay as the sensors.

* Checking the sample rate with the hardware
Delta-sigma modules (NI 9234, 9235, 9260, …) support only discrete rates, and modules sharing a
task must share one master timebase, so DAQmx may use a different rate than the one entered. All
input channels are in one task: every channel is sampled at the same rate and instant, and the app
always analyses with the rate DAQmx actually uses (it is logged at the start of a run).

Output Setup → /Sampling rate/ → *Check with hardware* asks DAQmx, without starting anything, which
input and output rate it would really use for the enabled channels and the drive channel. It then
shows:
- the actual input rate (marked when coerced, with a link to use it) and whether the output rate
  matches it exactly,
- the error, if DAQmx refuses the rate (e.g. 51.2 kHz with an NI 9235, which allows ≤ 10.24 kHz
  on the 9234's timebase),
- up to 12 nearby rates that every module accepts *exactly* — click one to use it.
Choosing such a rate makes the Output Setup previews (Δf, frequency plans, Nyquist warnings) match
the run exactly. In simulation mode the check only notes that the simulator uses the rate as entered.

* Hardware notes (cDAQ-9174)
- *Sample rates.* Delta-sigma input modules (NI 9234/9232/9250) only support discrete rates such
  as 51.2 kHz / n, and DAQmx coerces the rate you request. The app reads back the actual rate and
  uses it for the AO. If the AO module cannot produce exactly the same rate, the log shows a
  *warning*, because the excitation would then no longer be periodic in the block. Pick a rate
  both modules hit exactly (e.g. 25.6 kHz, 12.8 kHz, 6.4 kHz with a 9234 + 9263), or try
  /Synchronisation → AO clocked by AI sample clock/.
- *IEPE current.* The NI 9234 and 9250 supply 2 mA only. The 9232 supports 2 or 4 mA. Use AC
  coupling for IEPE sensors.
- *Filter delay.* The input module's filter delay does not affect the FRFs, because force and
  response are both measured on AI channels, which share the same delay.
- *Mixed delta-sigma modules.* Delta-sigma modules from different families (e.g. 9234 at
  51.2 kHz/n and 9237 at 50 kHz/n) cannot share one AI task. Put them in compatible combinations.
- *AO queue depth.* Over USB, keep blocks at about 50 ms or longer. If you see AO underflow
  errors on a busy PC, increase /AO queue depth/. This adds latency only, not gaps.

* Notes for Ubuntu (Wayland)
On GNOME/Wayland, Qt may print =qt.qpa.wayland.textinput: … Trying to disable … but 0x0 is focused=
when focus moves between widgets or dialogs. It is harmless (Qt's input-method layer, not the
app) and the app now mutes that one logging category at start-up. To see it again, start with
=QT_LOGGING_RULES="qt.qpa.wayland.textinput=true" python -m pymodalshaker=. To run under X11
(XWayland) instead: =QT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb python -m pymodalshaker=.
